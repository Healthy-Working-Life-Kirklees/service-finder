What this is

A proof of concept for a Digital Assistant that helps frontline staff, partners and local people find work and health support in Kirklees. You tell it a bit about the situation (age, work, the health issue getting in the way, nearest town) and it suggests schemes from a curated list, with contact details and how to get in.

It only recommends schemes that are in the list, and the contact details on every card come straight from the list, not from the AI. The point of this test is to find out whether the matching is good enough, whether the information is right, and what's missing.

How to use it

  1. Open the Service Finder. Choose I'm looking for support for myself or I'm helping someone else. The second gives shorter, more practical answers, with eligibility and referral points up front.
  2. Type a few lines, or tap an example. Age band, work situation, the kind of health issue and the nearest town are enough.
  3. You get a short reply and up to four scheme cards. Each card shows the contact details, how to get in, who can join, what happens and where, and when the information was last checked.
  4. Reply to answer its follow-up question, or press Start again to clear everything.
  5. Copy details on a card puts a plain-text summary on your clipboard, handy for sending to a client.

What good looks like

  • Every scheme named is real and on the list, and the contact details match the source document.
  • Eligibility is honest. It flags age limits and exclusions and doesn't claim to confirm that someone qualifies.
  • It's open about location. If a scheme has no base in the person's town, it says so.
  • It doesn't offer schemes built for a specific condition (substance use, severe mental illness, learning disability or autism) unless the person has said that applies.
  • Replies are short, in plain English, and don't ask for names, addresses or dates of birth.
  • It says "I don't know" or "this isn't covered" rather than guessing.
  • Crisis wording always gets the crisis response.

If it falls short of any of those, log it.

What it can't do yet

  • It doesn't check the live web. It knows only what's in the Aug 26 v2.1 pathways document. Nothing updates automatically.
  • It can't confirm someone is eligible, check availability or make a referral.
  • It works at town level only. No postcodes, deliberately.
  • Four entries are overdue for a check (see the data table). Their cards carry a warning.
  • The Pathways to Possibilities entries are missing a web page and a self-referral route. The card says so.
  • The AI isn't perfect. It can occasionally word things badly or ask a question when it could have just answered. That's what we're testing for.
  • There are limits to protect the budget: 10 messages a minute per person, and 100 chats a day across the whole tool. If you see "daily limit reached", try again tomorrow, or tell Phil.

How it works

  • The data is one file of 17 schemes (data/services.json), converted from the pathways document. The AI is given the whole list with each message.
  • The instructions that tell the AI how to behave are in prompt/system.md. We can change them without touching any code.
  • Cards are drawn from the data file. The AI only chooses which scheme IDs to show, how well each fits ("Strong match" or "Worth checking") and writes the short reason and what to check first. Anything not on the list is thrown away, and so is anything that isn't open yet.
  • Safeguards: schemes for a specific circumstance are only shown if the person's own words mention it. Crisis wording triggers the crisis response regardless of what the AI decides, and urgent replies never show scheme cards or ask questions. The AI can ask at most two follow-up questions in a chat, and the service counts them rather than trusting the AI to. Schemes that aren't open yet are never recommended.
  • The AI is Claude Sonnet 5.5, called through a small Cloudflare service that holds the key. The page is hosted on GitHub Pages.

Privacy while testing

  • Please test with made-up scenarios. Don't type real names, addresses, dates of birth or NHS numbers, and don't describe a real client in identifiable detail.
  • The finder doesn't store conversations. They exist only in your browser tab. Messages do pass through Cloudflare to Anthropic's Claude to get an answer, and these are third-party services.
  • The issues log does store what you write in it. It's protected by a shared passcode and kept on the same Cloudflare service. Keep entries free of personal information. The wording of a test message is fine, as long as it's made up.
  • The website and its code are public. Please don't share the link beyond the MVP team yet.

Reporting a problem

Use the issues and actions log. It's a shared list for the MVP team, protected by a passcode (ask Phil). A good entry says:

  1. What you typed (made-up, please), and which mode you were in.
  2. What you expected.
  3. What you got, and what was wrong with it.

Use Issue for something wrong, Action for something someone needs to do, Idea for something we could add, and Question for something we need to decide.

What has changed

  • 8 Oct 2026: new instructions for the AI. It now says how well each scheme fits ("Strong match" or "Worth checking") and what to check first. It asks at most two follow-up questions per chat. It recognises five kinds of urgent support, not just mental health crisis. Schemes now record who they are for, anything the person must already be using, and which health needs they cover.
  • 7 Oct 2026: first working version. The AI is Claude Sonnet 5.5. Added the crisis safeguard, the condition-specific scheme safeguard, "last checked" warnings, and this help page and log.
